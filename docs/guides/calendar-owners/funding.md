# Support your instance

A funding plan is a recurring contribution from you to the instance that hosts your calendar. It's for the community group, venue or neighborhood association that relies on the service and wants to help pay for it. Running an instance costs its operator real money: servers, storage, someone's time. Your plan helps cover that, closer to how public radio funds itself than to a paywall.

It's voluntary, and the free tier is complete. Without a plan you can still publish events, keep a public calendar page, follow other calendars and repost their events. Extended features are a thank-you to supporters, and the money goes to your instance, never to a calendar.

## What a plan costs

Your instance sets the price, and the price is per calendar. A plan that covers two calendars costs twice as much as a plan that covers one, and each calendar a plan covers is billed on the same monthly or yearly cycle.

Some instances run on pay what you can instead. There you choose your own amount when you start a plan, and the price you see is a suggestion.

You'll see the price for your instance before you commit to anything.

## What a plan unlocks

Today, one extended feature: [embedding your calendar on your own website](embed.md). Scheduled imports from outside calendars are planned but not available yet, so don't start a plan expecting them.

If you don't have a website to embed on, nothing else changes for your calendar when you start a plan. That's a fine reason to start one anyway, because supporting the instance is the point. It's also a fine reason not to.

## Start a plan

You need to own the calendar. An editor sees a note that the owner can do this instead.

1. Open **Settings → Calendar → General** with the calendar selected, and find the **Extended Features** section. The **Allowed Domain** section of the Widget screen offers the same thing.
2. Click <Btn>Contribute to enable</Btn>.
3. Choose monthly or yearly, or your amount on a pay-what-you-can instance, and confirm your contribution.
4. Enter your payment details in the form that appears.

The section changes to show the calendar as enabled once your payment is confirmed. The plan belongs to your account, and an account holds one plan at a time, however many calendars it covers.

::: tip <Lightbulb /> Monthly or yearly.
Pick the cycle you want to stay on. There's no switch between them in Pavillion: changing means cancelling, waiting for the plan to end, and starting a new one.
:::

## Cover another calendar

Add it to the plan you already have. You don't need a second plan or a second round of payment details.

Open the other calendar's **Extended Features** section. Instead of the offer to start a plan, you'll see what this calendar would add to your plan for your billing cycle. Click <Btn>Add this calendar to your plan</Btn>, and the calendar is covered straight away.

You aren't charged anything on the day. The new total starts at your next renewal, so the calendar is covered until then at no extra cost.

To stop covering a calendar, use <Btn>Disable extended features</Btn> in the same section. It stays covered until the end of the current billing period, and your total drops at the next renewal.

## Manage your plan

Everything else lives under **Settings → Account → Funding Plan**: your plan's status, its current billing period, the calendars it covers and what each one costs.

**Renewal** happens on its own at the end of each billing period, for as long as the plan continues.

**Payment details** are handled by Stripe, which runs the payment form and keeps your card details, so they never reach your instance or Pavillion. <Btn>Manage Payment Method</Btn> takes you to Stripe's billing page, where the plan shows as a recurring subscription.

**Cancelling** takes effect at the end of the billing period you've already paid for, and your extended features continue until then. If you change your mind before that date, <Btn>Keep My Plan</Btn> withdraws the cancellation and the plan carries on as if nothing happened.

Your calendars stay online whether or not you have a plan. When coverage ends, your public calendar page carries on as before, but an embed on your own website stops showing your events.

## Things that trip people up

**Removing the last calendar cancels the plan.** A plan with nothing to cover can't continue, so disabling extended features on its only calendar cancels the whole plan at the end of the billing period. If you're moving coverage from one calendar to another, add the new one first.

**A failed payment doesn't end anything straight away.** Your calendars stay covered through a grace period set by your instance, and you'll get an email. If payment still hasn't gone through when the grace period ends, the plan is suspended and coverage stops. Update your payment method from the Funding Plan page to bring it back. You can't start a second plan or add a calendar until the first one is paid up.

**You can't add a calendar to a plan you've cancelled.** Until the end date you'll be pointed to the Funding Plan page. Use <Btn>Keep My Plan</Btn> first if you want to cover more calendars, or wait for the plan to end and start a new one.

**A calendar may be covered without a plan.** If its section says **Complimentary grant**, your instance's operator has covered it for you and there's nothing for you to start or cancel.

**Your editors can't see your plan.** Only you see its state, including a failed payment, so if embedding stops working for an editor, they'll need to ask you.
